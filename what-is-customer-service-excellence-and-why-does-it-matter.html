Sales & Customer Service · A Question We Hear Often

What is customer service excellence, and why does it matter?

By Banio Luiji Nobert · Potential Unexplored Ltd

Most organisations think they already have good customer service, and often they do, on an ordinary day, with an ordinary customer, asking an ordinary question. Excellence tends to reveal itself somewhere else entirely, and honestly, in more than one place.

One place it shows up is in how staff handle a genuinely frustrated customer, without becoming defensive, without making the customer feel like a problem, and without letting the interaction escalate simply because nobody was equipped to de-escalate it calmly.

"Anyone can serve a happy customer well. Excellence shows up in how you serve the frustrated one."

Another place it shows up is in memory and continuity, whether a returning customer has to re-explain their situation from scratch every time, or whether the organisation has actually held onto what happened last time and picked up the thread with care.

It shows up too in how mistakes get handled, not whether mistakes happen, because they always will somewhere, but whether the recovery feels genuine or scripted, whether a customer leaves that conversation feeling like the company actually cares, or simply like they followed a policy correctly.

And there is a quieter place it shows up that businesses often underestimate, in how valued and equipped the staff themselves feel. A team that is anxious, undertrained, or unsure whether their manager will back them up in a difficult interaction tends to become guarded rather than warm, and customers feel that guardedness immediately, even if they cannot name what they are picking up on.

Building real customer service excellence usually means paying attention to all of these places at once, not just the moment of the transaction itself, but the memory behind it, the recovery after something goes wrong, and the wellbeing of the people delivering it. Whichever one your organisation has been neglecting is usually the one your customers are quietly noticing most.
