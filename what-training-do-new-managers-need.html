Leadership · A Question We Hear Often

What training do new managers need?

By Banio Luiji Nobert · Potential Unexplored Ltd

Short answer. New managers need training in self-leadership, delegation, giving and receiving feedback, holding difficult conversations, running good meetings, coaching their team and managing performance. They also need help leading former peers. Training works best when it starts within the first months of promotion and continues with coaching over time, rather than one workshop.

Most managers in Uganda are promoted because they were excellent at their job. The best accountant becomes head of finance. The top loan officer becomes branch manager. Then, almost overnight, success depends on a completely different set of skills, and very few organisations train for those skills before handing over the role.

The skills that matter most in the first year

  • Self-leadership. Managing their own time, emotions and example, because the team now watches everything they do.
  • Delegation. Letting go of the work they used to do well and trusting others to do it, even differently.
  • Feedback. Praising specifically and correcting early, without avoiding hard truths or crushing people.
  • Difficult conversations. Addressing lateness, poor performance or conflict calmly and fairly.
  • Leading former peers. Setting new boundaries with colleagues who used to be friends at the same level.
  • Running meetings. Short, clear meetings that end with decisions and owners.
  • Coaching. Asking good questions so team members find solutions themselves.
  • Performance management. Setting clear expectations, tracking progress and reporting upwards.

Why one workshop is rarely enough

A single training day can open a new manager's eyes, but new habits form through practice. The most effective approach combines group sessions with one to one coaching over several months, so managers try new skills at work, reflect on what happened and try again with support. Pairing new managers with an experienced mentor inside the organisation also helps.

When to start

The best time is just before or within the first three months of promotion, before unhelpful habits settle in. If your organisation promotes several people a year, consider a yearly new managers programme so every cohort starts on the same foundation.

At Potential Unexplored Ltd we start every leadership programme with self-leadership, then move to people leadership, using practical tools such as our CIVEC Trust Framework and our 6R Coaching Conversation Framework.

"Promotion changes a person's title overnight. Training and coaching are what change how they lead."
