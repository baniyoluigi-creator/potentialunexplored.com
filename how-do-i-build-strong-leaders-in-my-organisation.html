Leadership · A Question We Hear Often

How do I build strong leaders inside my organisation?

By Banio Luiji Nobert · Potential Unexplored Ltd

This question comes to me in different disguises, but underneath it is always the same worry. A good manager has just left, or a critical role has stayed empty for months because nobody internally felt ready for it, and the organisation is now asking itself whether it has been growing leaders at all or simply hoping some would appear.

There is no one answer here, and I get suspicious of anyone who gives you a single formula. What I have seen work is usually a combination of a few different things, and which combination matters most depends on your organisation, your industry, and honestly, the personalities already sitting inside your team.

Sometimes the missing piece is simply identification. Organisations promote the loudest or most visible person, the one who speaks up in meetings, and quietly overlook someone steadier who has been carrying real ownership without ever asking for credit for it. Widening who you are watching for leadership potential, and watching for it earlier than the promotion conversation, changes a great deal on its own.

"You cannot delegate confidence, and you cannot promote your way into self-awareness."

Sometimes the gap is exposure. A person can only grow as a leader as fast as the responsibility in front of them stretches, and many talented employees spend years doing the same scope of work because nobody handed them something slightly too big for them yet, with enough support that failing at it would not be a disaster.

Sometimes, and this is the one most organisations skip entirely, the missing piece is internal. Someone can have every external opportunity, the exposure, the title, the mentorship, and still struggle, because nobody ever helped them build a working relationship with their own habits, their own identity, their own sense of purpose. A person who has not learned to lead themselves tends to borrow confidence from the room rather than bring it, and that shows up quickly once the pressure is real.

And sometimes it is simpler still. It is culture. An organisation that quietly punishes mistakes will never grow leaders who are willing to make the small, necessary ones that come with learning to decide for a team rather than for yourself. People rise to the level of risk they feel safe taking.

If you are trying to build leaders inside your own organisation, it is worth asking honestly which of these you have actually been doing, and which you have been assuming would just happen on its own. Usually it is more than one gap at a time, and closing even two of them changes the trajectory faster than most people expect.
