Leadership and Management Development

Leadership is a capacity, not a title. We work with managers, founders, directors, and executives starting where most leadership development never goes: with the person before the role. Self-leadership comes first. Before a manager can lead others with credibility, they must be able to lead themselves with honesty. People leadership follows.

  • Self-awareness and emotional intelligence: understanding what drives your decisions and your leadership style
  • Communication and influence: speaking in ways people hear, listening in ways that make people feel understood
  • Coaching skills for managers: drawing performance out of people rather than demanding it
  • Building accountability cultures: environments where commitments are taken seriously
  • Conflict resolution and constructive challenge: addressing disagreement before it becomes dysfunction
  • Leading through change and uncertainty: keeping people focused when the environment is shifting
  • Strategy, vision and organisational direction: clarity about where you are going and how to communicate it
  • Delegation, reporting and performance management: assigning work in ways that develop capacity
  • Work-life balance as a leadership responsibility: modelling sustainable pace across the team
  • Executive mentoring: sustained private one-to-one growth relationship for senior leaders

Past engagements include Search for Common Ground (South Sudan, 2023), NASHO Computers, Bank of Uganda, and Muni University. Engagements range from a single day to a year-long partnership.

Leadership training in Uganda

Leadership development that changes how managers lead

Potential Unexplored Ltd designs and facilitates leadership and management development for organisations across Uganda and Africa. We work with new managers, middle managers, senior leadership teams, founders and executive directors. Our starting point is self-leadership, because people follow a manager they trust, and trust begins with how a leader manages himself or herself.

In short. We offer one day leadership workshops, two to three day leadership retreats, and six to ten month leadership programmes with monthly sessions and one to one coaching, delivered in person and online. Current and past programmes include a ten month programme for 33 Opportunity International managers in nine countries and a year long programme for 12 managers at NASHO Computers.

Who it is for

Leaders we develop

New managers

People recently promoted from doing the work to leading others, who need skills in delegation, feedback and difficult conversations.

Middle managers

Managers who carry the weight between senior leadership and frontline staff, and need to lead, coach and report well.

Senior leadership teams

Directors and heads of department working on strategy, culture, change and how they lead together.

Founders and executive directors

Leaders who want a private thinking partner through executive mentoring, often alongside a programme for their managers.

NGO and partner leaders

Executive directors and managers of partner organisations, often brought together by a funding organisation.

Formats

How we deliver leadership development

One day leadership workshop

An eye opening day on one or two priority themes

MorningSelf-leadership, using the Progress Triangle of Personal, People and Practical Mastery
MiddayA priority theme, for example trust, accountability or coaching your team
AfternoonPractice through role plays and real cases, then personal action plans

Two or three day leadership retreat

Often combined with team building, for example African Women Rising's three day retreat for senior managers

Day 1Self-leadership, values and personal effectiveness
Day 2People leadership, including trust, communication, accountability and conflict
Day 3Leading the organisation forward, with plans, commitments and follow up

Six to ten month leadership programme

Our most effective format for lasting change

LaunchAn in person or online launch, a self assessment and personal goals for each manager
Each monthA group session of about two hours on one theme, with practical assignments
In betweenOne to one coaching conversations, progress tracking and short feedback forms
CloseA final review, evaluation and a report to the organisation

Curriculum

Sample themes in a longer programme

Themes are chosen with each organisation. A typical programme moves from leading yourself to leading people to leading the organisation, for example:

  • Self-leadership, emotional intelligence and personal effectiveness
  • Communication and influence
  • Building trust and accountability in teams
  • Coaching and developing others
  • Strategic thinking
  • Team dynamics, collaboration and conflict resolution
  • Decision making and leading through change
  • Delegation, reporting and performance management
  • Work life balance as a leadership responsibility
  • Strategic leadership and vision execution

Our approach

What makes our leadership development different

  • Self-leadership first. Before a manager leads others with credibility, he or she must lead themselves with honesty. Every programme starts there.
  • Coaching, not only teaching. We use our 6R Coaching Conversation Framework (Rapport, Reality, Risk, Reach, Routes, Resolve) in one to one sessions and teach managers to coach their own teams.
  • Practical tools. Managers leave with tools they can use the next day, such as the CIVEC Trust Framework, the Team Accountability Compass and simple self assessments.
  • Accountability between sessions. Monthly progress tracking, personal coaching letters and feedback forms keep learning alive between sessions.
  • Reports you can use. The organisation receives regular progress reports with observations and recommendations.

How we work with you

From first conversation to follow up

Every engagement begins with a conversation. We listen to what is happening with your managers and leaders, what has already been tried, and what you want to see change. Only then do we shape the programme, so that it fits your people and is not a copy of what we did for someone else.

  1. Listen. A short call or visit to understand your people, your setting and your goals.
  2. Propose. A written proposal with the content, the days, the venue needs and the cost, in shillings.
  3. Deliver. Participatory sessions led by our facilitators, with real stories, exercises and time to practise.
  4. Follow up. A short report and a check in after the sessions, so the learning is not lost when everyone returns to work.
